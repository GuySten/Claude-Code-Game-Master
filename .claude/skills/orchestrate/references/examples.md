# Worked examples

The first four pieces written for the table, and what made each one work. The
full scores are in the campaign's `music/arrangements/` folder (the Striding Keep
campaign in the dnd-campaigns repo).

## 1. A barbarian's legendary theme (6/8, C# Mixolydian, an ostinato tune)

The tune: five repeated tonics then a leap to the 5th (the hook), A A' B A form,
a climb to a held high 5th at bar 11.

- **Intro (2 bars):** timpani and low strings play the hook's *rhythm* (five
  C#s, then G#) - foreshadowing before anyone plays the tune; tremolo strings and
  a low open fifth (trombone + tuba: horns can't go that low) swell in bar 2.
- **A:** four horns alone over a cello pulse (doubled by bassoons for bite).
  Chords I - IV - I/E# - bVII: the Mixolydian bVII makes it a barbarian rather
  than a knight.
- **A':** violins an octave up (with flutes and a clarinet giving their quick
  notes an attack), horns move to a rising countermelody beneath.
- **B, the climb:** a climbing bass (vi - bVII - v - I - bVII - v - I), snare in
  the hook's rhythm getting louder each bar, trumpets join for the last climb,
  timpani + snare roll into...
- **The climax:** the held top note over **E major (bIII)** instead of home - the
  chromatic-mediant lift - with choir on that chord and a cymbal crash.
- **Home:** the timpani play the hook's rhythm again (a callback), choir sings the
  tune with horns, trumpets, violins and flutes; bVI - IV - bVII - I with a
  ritardando into a timpani roll and a final stroke.

The host's verdict: "a lot better" than the rule-based arrangement - the
difference was the tune passed around, the countermelody, the planned climax
surprise and the ending.

## 2. The same barbarian's dark twin (the --minor tune, C# Phrygian)

- Slower (dotted quarter 50), a war march: taiko on 1 and 4, the tune in low
  cellos + bassoons + horns an octave down; later horns + trombones low.
- Harmony: i, iv, then the **bII** under the tune's b2 and **V** (borrowed) under
  its sigh (b6 -> 5); the climb's tritone over **i°**; the climax on **bVI**.
- A second violin line descends over the brass statement - a lament for who he
  was. The end: bII - i Phrygian cadence, a stroke; then, alone, an oboe plays
  the *heroic* hook (five C#s, a G#) faintly.

## 3. An aristocratic villain's theme (4/4, C# Aeolian, a loop)

A tired, elegant lord of a walking fortress.
- The fortress's stride under everything: timpani on 1, a soft bass drum on 1
  and 3; pizzicato root on 1 and fifth on 3 - a pendulum. A soft bell tolls at
  the loop's start.
- The tune first on a solo english horn (shadowed quietly by a clarinet for its
  quick notes), then violins an octave up with cellos an octave down.
- The climax: the tune's tritone harmonized as **bVI7** (an augmented sixth) ->
  **i/G#** -> **V**, with organ chords, choir, tolling bells and a cymbal on the
  top note.
- The english horn returns, weary, and the stride carries it back to the start.

## 4. A brute's boss fight (3/4, A# Phrygian, a loop at 132)

- Vamp (intro and break): **i | bII** per bar; brass stab on each downbeat;
  choir holds a full-bar chord ("AAAH") per bar; violins2 (+ clarinets) play a
  Phrygian eighth-note ostinato; taiko, bass drum, timpani.
- Statement 1: horns + trombones (an octave down) on the tune; choir chords
  under it; cello eighths (+ bassoons) driving; snare on beats 2 and 3.
- Break: the vamp with toms, a timpani + snare roll into statement 2.
- Statement 2: trumpets, horns, trombones, violins an octave up, tuba two
  octaves down; the choir wide in two octaves; crashes on the climaxes.

The lesson from this one: the first version's choir was inaudible (its recording
is ~19 dB quieter than the horns', and its chant notes were too short to bloom).
Held, full-bar choir chords and an evened-out mix made it "a lot better".
